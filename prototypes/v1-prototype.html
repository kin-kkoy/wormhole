The Reluctant Commander

Aldric Thorne did not ask to wear the crimson sash. When his father fell at the Crosswind Bridge during the last Kyrathan offensive, Aldric was twenty-six and already weary of war. King Mardus III pressed the command upon him anyway, and Aldric — ever dutiful — accepted.

He commands the garrison of Valdris Keep with a quiet discipline that unsettles his peers. Where other captains roar, Aldric speaks softly. Where they drink, he reads. His men follow him not because they fear him, but because they cannot imagine disappointing him.

Between Two Oaths

In the weeks before the Treaty of Crosswind, Aldric was spotted in the Kyrathan city of Ironhaven — out of uniform, unaccompanied, meeting with someone the palace records refuse to name. The matter has never been raised publicly. Lyra Vex knows. She has said nothing.

Weapon

He carries Morrowlight, a plain longsword forged from river-iron. It has no name-magic, no runes. Aldric refuses ceremonial weapons on principle — "a sword is for ending, not for display." The blade is chipped in three places. He has never replaced it.

Treaty of Crosswind
Untitled Draft

The Crimson Age began with a broken oath and ended, four generations later, on the wet stones of Crosswind Bridge. Between those two moments, the kingdoms of Valdris and Kyrath bled each other nearly dry.

The First Kindling

It is traditional to blame Queen Berinna the Iron-Mouthed for starting the wars, though scholars in Silverdale dispute this. What is certain is that in the year 812 of the Common Reckoning, Berinna ordered the Kyrathan garrisons of the Crownfield to cross the River Thren without warning. The villages of the river valley were burned before the Valdrisi even learned their borders had been violated.

By spring of 813, King Hadrian Thorne had raised every able-bodied subject between the Grey Coast and the Ironspine. The response was not a counter-invasion — it was an extermination. Three Kyrathan villages were razed for every Valdrisi hamlet burned. Hadrian is said to have personally nailed Kyrathan banners to the doors of his hall. He wore their colors as gloves.

"We do not forgive. We do not forget. We remember the names of every smithy, every child, every dog. We write them in our blood. We read them to our sons."
— attributed to Hadrian Thorne, 813 CR

The Generations Between

The wars did not end. They simply grew old. Every monarch for four generations inherited the conflict as they inherited the crown, each one promising to finish what the last could not. The battle lines barely shifted. Only the size of the graveyards grew.

It was Aldric Thorne, commanding the garrison at Valdris Keep, who first wrote openly of peace — in a private letter to his father, two weeks before his father's death. The letter was never answered.
